Overview

Symmetric encryption and authenticated encryption

Parties that share a secret key encrypt data so that it stays confidential and any tampering is detected, using block ciphers, stream ciphers, or permutations inside modes of operation.

What it is

A nonce-based scheme for authenticated encryption with associated data (AEAD) encrypts a message M under a key K, a nonce N, and associated data A, producing a ciphertext that includes an authentication tag. Decryption with the same (K,N,A) returns M or rejects. The associated data, such as a packet header, is authenticated but not encrypted. Security combines privacy (ciphertexts look like random strings) and authenticity (no adversary produces a new ciphertext that decrypts), against adversaries that never repeat a nonce when encrypting under one key (). AEAD schemes are built from block ciphers, from stream ciphers, or from public permutations.

Core notions

  • Block cipher: a keyed permutation on fixed-size blocks, required to be a pseudorandom permutation (; historically ).
  • Confidentiality-only modes: CBC with an unpredictable IV and CTR with unique counter blocks () give IND-CPA security but no integrity; their ciphertexts are malleable.
  • Authenticated encryption (AE): privacy together with integrity of ciphertexts. The two together imply security against chosen-ciphertext attacks.
  • Nonce: a value that must not repeat under one key; it need not be secret or random. In counter-based modes a repeated nonce reveals the XOR of plaintexts and can enable forgeries.
  • Misuse-resistant AE: if a nonce repeats, the only leakage is whether the same (N,A,M) was encrypted twice. Synthetic-IV (SIV) designs derive the IV from a MAC of all inputs, at the cost of a second pass (, ).
  • Key commitment: a ciphertext should decrypt correctly under at most one key. It is not implied by AE security: AES-GCM, ChaCha20-Poly1305, AES-GCM-SIV, and OCB3 admit ciphertexts valid under two keys (). Stronger variants also commit to the nonce and associated data, and context-discovery attacks break permissive commitment for CCM, EAX, and SIV ().
  • Online, parallelizable, one-pass: structural properties that determine streaming and hardware cost.
  • Lightweight AEAD: designs for constrained devices, here a duplex mode over a small public permutation ().

Main approaches

  • Block cipher with CTR and a MAC: CCM pairs CTR with CBC-MAC (), EAX with OMAC (), and GCM with a polynomial hash over GF(2128) ().
  • Stream cipher with a one-time authenticator: ChaCha () combined with Poly1305 ().
  • Synthetic IV: AES-SIV () and AES-GCM-SIV, which also derives per-nonce keys ().
  • Permutation-based: Ascon (), standardized as Ascon-AEAD128 ().
  • Committing transforms: generic ways to add key or context commitment with limited ciphertext expansion ().

This topic follows the path from block and stream ciphers through confidentiality modes to authenticated, misuse-resistant, and lightweight AEAD, including standards that fix interoperable constructions and analyses of these constructions. Standalone hashes and MACs, disk encryption, format-preserving encryption, key wrap, and performance rankings are outside it.
