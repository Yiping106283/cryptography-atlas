Overview

Proof systems and succinct arguments

Protocols by which a prover convinces a verifier that a statement is true, possibly revealing nothing else and with proofs far shorter than the computation they certify.

What it is

A proof system for an NP relation R lets a prover holding an instance x and a witness w with (x,w)∈R convince a verifier that x belongs to the language. Completeness: the honest verifier accepts. Soundness: no prover makes the verifier accept a false statement, except with small probability; it is statistical for proofs and computational for arguments. Knowledge soundness strengthens this: an extractor recovers a witness from any convincing prover. Zero knowledge: a simulator produces the verifier's view from x alone ().

Core notions

  • Interactive, public-coin, non-interactive: in a public-coin protocol the verifier sends only random challenges. The Fiat–Shamir transform replaces them by hashes of the transcript, with soundness argued in the random-oracle model (). Non-interactive zero knowledge (NIZK) can also use a common reference string (, ).
  • SNARG and SNARK: a succinct non-interactive argument for a circuit C has proof size poly(λ,log∣C∣) and verification time poly(λ,∣x∣,log∣C∣), possibly after preprocessing. A SNARK is a SNARG of knowledge; a zkSNARK is also zero knowledge.
  • Setup: a circuit-specific structured reference string from a trusted ceremony (); a universal, updatable one reused across circuits (, ); or a transparent setup that uses only public randomness ().
  • PCP and IOP: in a probabilistically checkable proof (PCP) the verifier reads a few positions of a long proof. An interactive oracle proof (IOP) allows several rounds of such oracles; in a polynomial IOP the oracles are polynomials. Kilian commits to a PCP with a Merkle tree to obtain a succinct argument ().
  • Polynomial commitment scheme (PCS): commit to a polynomial, then prove its evaluations at chosen points. Instances: pairing-based KZG with constant-size openings and a trusted setup (), discrete-log inner-product arguments (), hash-based Reed–Solomon proximity tests (), and lattice schemes ().
  • Arithmetization: the constraint language for the computation, such as R1CS/QAP, AIR, PLONKish constraints, or CCS. Sum-check () is the basic protocol for multilinear statements.
  • Recursion, accumulation, folding: incrementally verifiable computation (IVC) and proof-carrying data (PCD) prove long computations step by step. Accumulation () and folding () defer expensive checks to one final decider.
  • Simulation extractability: knowledge soundness that survives the adversary seeing simulated proofs; it rules out mauling proofs.

Main approaches

  • Pairing-based QAP SNARKs: three-group-element proofs with a circuit-specific setup ().
  • Polynomial IOP plus PCS: universal-setup systems compiled with KZG (, ).
  • Hash-based coded oracles: transparent, plausibly post-quantum systems from Reed–Solomon proximity testing and Merkle commitments (, ).
  • Inner-product arguments: logarithmic proofs without trusted setup, but linear-time verification for general circuits (); recursion without trusted setup ().
  • Sum-check and multilinear systems: transparent R1CS arguments () and lookup-based zkVMs (, ).
  • Folding: , , .
  • Security of deployed configurations: knowledge soundness of PLONK in the random-oracle model from falsifiable assumptions (), simulation extractability of universal zkSNARKs (), and post-quantum soundness of IOP-based arguments ().

This topic covers general-purpose proofs and arguments: interactive and Fiat–Shamir-compiled systems, pairing, discrete-log, and hash-based backends, arithmetizations, polynomial commitments, lookups, recursion, folding, and zkVMs, plus security analyses of concrete configurations. Quantum proofs, designated-verifier proofs, MPC-in-the-head, and lattice proof systems are not yet covered as subfields.
